Globalizing Access to Homeownership

An International Collateral Guarantee Architecture

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper develops an international institutional architecture for expanding access to owner-occupied housing through contingent collateral support rather than unconditional purchase grants. The proposed International Housing Guarantee Institution (IHGI) supplements collateral capacity for households with adequate repayment ability but insufficient accumulated wealth, while domestic regulated lenders retain responsibility for origination, servicing and a positive share of loss. A household model separates liquidity and income constraints; a capped indemnity contract defines the allocation of verified losses; a spatial equilibrium model identifies conditions under which credit expansion is capitalized into housing prices; and a multilateral portfolio model characterizes the limits of diversification under correlated shocks. The empirical benchmark reconstructs descriptive measures from the United Kingdom’s 2021 Mortgage Guarantee Scheme administrative statistics, covering 58,856 completions through June 2025, and relates them to published quasi-experimental evidence on housing credit and supply. A reproducible synthetic population and correlated-default simulation examine access, affordability and tail capital sensitivity. The analysis argues for supply-linked commitments, independent affordability tests, lender risk retention, local-currency loans, transparent fiscal concessions and an evaluation design centered on durable incremental ownership. The proposed institution’s effects remain unestimated: administrative counts are not causal effects and simulations are conditional illustrations rather than observed outcomes.
