The Economics of Aesthetic Experience

Aesthetic Opportunity and the Design of Entitlements, Markets, and Institutions

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Welfare & Capabilities39
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

Access to aesthetic experience is shaped jointly by income, information, geography, scarce capacity, and the institutions through which consumers and suppliers meet. This paper develops a marketdesign framework for luxury-purpose currency and aesthetic basic income, interpreted as restricted purchasing rights embedded in a competitive system of experience provision. The analysis separates intrinsic enjoyment from positional value, nominal balances from attainable opportunities, and issuer authorization from credible settlement. It establishes a cash-dominance benchmark and then studies departures arising from distributional objectives, external effects, supply commitments, private information, and dynamic preference discovery. A sequence of tractable models characterizes subsidy incidence with universal and targeted eligibility, incentivecompatible procurement, the option value of learning, rollover, capacity choice under uncertain demand, spatial allocation, platform dependence, and correlated redemption risk. The results identify both the potential gains from integrating portable entitlements with verifiable supply and the conditions under which restrictions merely transfer rents or reduce household discretion. Formal propositions, proofs, numerical illustrations, and an evaluation design connect institutional choices to observable outcomes. The paper does not infer welfare from redemption or treat restricted issuance as a source of real resources. Its central conclusion is that experiential access is an implementability problem: a credible entitlement must jointly satisfy household choice, supplier incentives, physical feasibility, and fiscal coverage, and must outperform feasible alternatives under an explicit welfare criterion.
