Invisible Welfare

Protecting Dignity through Privacy-Preserving, Predictive, and Scalable Social Protection

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Welfare & Capabilities18
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

Welfare institutions often require people to reveal their poverty in order to obtain relief from it. This paper develops a privacy-preserving, predictive, and scalable alternative that identifies legally eligible needs, minimizes repeated disclosure, and delivers support through ordinary transactions without marking recipients to merchants or bystanders. The proposal shares Banerjee and Duflo’s normative commitment to dignity, agency, and the lived circumstances of the poor, while distinguishing intensive human-centered learning and care from the routine delivery technology appropriate for millions. A formal model combines heterogeneous take-up, stigma, disclosure risk, targeting error, choice, supply responses, and economies of scale. It derives conditions favoring invisible automatic provision and conditions favoring personintensive or universal alternatives. Verified findings are separated from a transparent synthetic simulation. The empirical agenda specifies factorial trials, quasi-experimental rollouts, market spillover analysis, and independent privacy audits. Rather than assuming that algorithmic prediction is benign, the paper treats wrongful exclusion, digital disadvantage, surveillance, and loss of agency as binding constraints. The contribution is a testable institutional framework, not a claim that automation has already been shown to outperform existing social protection.
