Global Sovereign Debt Resets and Fiscal Resilience

Coordinated Creditor Redemption, Contingent Fiscal Reserves, and Inflation-Constrained Recovery

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Fiscal & Financial Stability16
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper evaluates a treaty-based, exceptional international sovereign debt reset in which eligible creditor claims are redeemed in their lawful currencies and public issuance rights are separated from issued conventional monetary liabilities and genuinely funded national fiscal reserves. The program is considered only when routine debt-management tools, including verified productivity-linked purpose-bound fiscal substitution, are insufficient under a transparent social-welfare and debt-distress test. It is not a claim that recorded government bonds can be extinguished without creating other obligations or real resource costs. The revised architecture explicitly separates three mutually compatible, but institutionally independent, instruments: ordinary productivity-backed purpose-bound money (PoC) used to replace verifiable fiscal spending and retire bonds gradually; a sectoral PoC conversion rule used to manage PoC-related temporary price pressure without breaching provider contracts; and a distinct extraordinary conventional-monetary redemption window used in a sovereign crisis. The article develops stock-flow accounting that prohibits counting an already retired security twice, preserves creditor contractual claims, and differentiates equal nominal issuance rights from unequal safe execution rates. It incorporates bond collateral scarcity, currency mismatch, cashdemand inflation, investor expectations, real-income distribution, provider settlement liabilities, reserve-fund withdrawal limits, and dynamic trigger incentives. A transitory increase in inflation is not mechanically treated as failure when medium-term household real purchasing power and production improve, but durable inflation or eroded essential access can rule out issuance. Official fiscal and debt evidence remains descriptive, while all reset-specific numerical simulations are transparently hypothetical. The result is a falsifiable conditional framework, not a demonstrated gain from universal periodic debt monetization.
