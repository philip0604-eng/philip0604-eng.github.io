Monetary Design and Inflation

A Structural Theory of Price Stability

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

Abstract

The price consequences of monetary expansion depend on the expenditure opportunities created by issuance and the capacity of the economy to meet the resulting demand. This paper develops a structural framework for purpose-bound currency (PoC), a restricted purchasing instrument that can be redeemed only for specified goods and services. Unlike a universal claim about money neutrality, the analysis distinguishes the legal non-convertibility of an instrument from the economic interdependence of markets. A household model with separate spending constraints, a capacity-constrained supply system, and an explicit settlement mechanism identify conditions under which additional purchasing rights increase access without raising transaction prices. These conditions can fail through displacement of ordinary expenditure, shared inputs, provider market power, redemption into unrestricted money, or limited supply. A multisector comparative-static system characterizes direct and indirect price responses; a dynamic issuance problem incorporates investment delays, stochastic capacity, model uncertainty, and price safeguards. Reproducible numerical experiments compare restricted and unrestricted transfers at equal nominal allocations and examine the trade-off between access and price stability. Evidence from randomized cash-versus-in-kind transfers and retail studies of food benefits supports the relevance of supply and spending composition, while remaining insufficient to identify the causal effect of the proposed monetary architecture. A descriptive analysis of observed US manufacturing utilization and producer prices from 2000 to 2025 highlights the limits of aggregate slack measures. A pilot-based empirical design specifies the data and variation necessary for causal identification. The contribution is a conditional theory of monetary design, not a claim that monetary quantities or macroeconomic constraints cease to matter.
