Redesigning Shareholder Capitalism

A New Theory of Capital Allocation and Social Value

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

The paper develops a conditional theory of purpose-oriented capital allocation within a shareholder-based market economy. Conventional markets aggregate information about expected monetizable returns, but important external consequences of innovation remain outside firm cash flows. We model an auxiliary financing channel that combines commercial returns with independently estimated social contributions under a binding aggregate resource constraint. For a two-project economy, we derive an exact welfare comparison and show that the optimal weight on purpose information declines as measurement error increases. In a multiproject environment, strategic disclosure, audit costs, dynamic innovation spillovers, and correlated evaluation feedback can reverse the gains from purpose-directed investment. The framework separates contractual financial claims from verified social-outcome claims, emphasizing additional real capital formation, budget balance, and the legitimacy of heterogeneous welfare preferences. An observed-data exercise re-estimates standard investment equations using 220 historical firm-year observations from the Grunfeld panel; these data do not identify any purpose-finance effect. A distinct computational experiment simulates 450 economies of 120 projects and illustrates a model-dependent welfare gain of 6.20 percent under accurate signals, falling to a loss of 10.50 percent under severe noise. The central empirical agenda is an independently audited, preregistered experiment isolating information from financial subsidy effects. The proposal is neither a general rejection of shareholder capitalism nor a claim that social value can be reduced to an objective universal score. Its contribution is a set of falsifiable conditions under which social-purpose information may improve, or worsen, decentralized investment and innovation decisions.
