From Coercion to Design

Labor Markets, Subsistence, and the Transition Problem

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Welfare & Capabilities10
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper reexamines labor supply and welfare through the lens of subsistence constraints and develops a theory of economic transitions when such constraints are removed. In standard models, labor supply is treated as a continuous choice governed by marginal trade-offs. We show that this framework breaks down once baseline consumption is guaranteed: labor supply becomes a participation problem characterized by a threshold condition, and the observed equilibrium reflects a distorted outcome sustained by implicit coercion. Specifically, when subsistence binds, the equilibrium wage lies below the true reservation wage, generating a gap that is not priced in market outcomes. Eliminating subsistence constraints does not lead to a smooth adjustment to a new equilibrium. Instead, it creates a discontinuity: participation collapses if compensation remains below the reservation threshold. We formalize this as a transition problem and show that standard policy responses—such as full internalization or static subsidies—either induce collapse or generate persistent distortions. We propose a state-contingent, self-extinguishing policy rule that bridges this gap. The rule ensures participation by guaranteeing that effective compensation meets the reservation threshold, while preserving incentives for wage adjustment and technological change. We prove that the resulting dynamics are monotone and convergent: wages and productivity increase, the gap shrinks, and subsidies vanish endogenously. The mechanism is robust to heterogeneity, multi-sector environments, and automation, and can be embedded in a DSGE framework with non-convex labor supply. The main contribution of the paper is to shift the focus of economic analysis from equilibrium selection to path design. Labor markets are not neutral outcomes of preferences and technology but are shaped by institutional constraints. Once these constraints change, equilibrium becomes a function of the transition path. This paper provides a framework for designing such paths, transforming labor supply from a coercion-based equilibrium into a system governed by explicit design.
