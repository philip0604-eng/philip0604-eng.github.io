Market Design for a Federated Purpose-Bound Monetary System

Sovereign Participation, Corporate Coalitions, and Credible Settlement

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Global Development21
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper studies the implementation of a federated system of purpose oriented currencies in which a limited mandate Global Central Bank, participating sovereigns, and competing corporate coalitions jointly organize access to designated goods and services. A purpose restriction is modeled as a constraint on the exercise of an entitlement, not as an exemption from resource scarcity or a substitute for settlement funding. The framework distinguishes beneficiary balances, verified merchant receivables, and the funding commitments that discharge those receivables. It integrates household expenditure substitution, differentiated service competition, supplier screening, investment in delivery capacity, sovereign participation, and contingent loss allocation. Conditional implementation results establish when funded entitlement portfolios can be supported by incentive compatible supply contracts and individually rational participation transfers. Separate results characterize the gains from an expanded choice set, the financing and informational limits of those gains, and the conditions under which withdrawal remains credible without undermining outstanding claims. A network formulation connects sectoral demand to common production inputs, while a stochastic control problem makes issuance depend jointly on available capacity, fiscal backing, and verification quality. The extended analysis develops recursive household choice, a small general equilibrium closure, multiproduct competition, coalition bargaining and investment, sovereign private information and limited commitment, network clearing, constrained dynamic issuance, multitask verification, and congested service assignment. Reproducible numerical exercises characterize changes in binding constraints, sustainable insurance, settlement propagation, and timing dependent feedback stability. The central contribution is an integrated implementability frontier: public and private participation can be mutually reinforcing, but a hybrid system dominates feasible alternatives only when its gains from coordination, innovation, and preference matching exceed its real operating costs, financing distortions, informational rents, and settlement risks.
