Recursive Coordination Capitalism

Programmable Demand Architectures and Innovation Governance in AI-Scale Economies

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper develops a theoretical framework for understanding purpose-bound money (PBM) as a programmable coordination architecture for AI-scale economies. Unlike conventional monetary systems, which primarily function as generalized exchange and stabilization mechanisms, PBM systems can structure future demand, influence technological diffusion, reduce investment uncertainty, and coordinate long-term innovation dynamics. Building on endogenous growth theory, Schumpeterian creative destruction, capability theory, and computational governance, the paper introduces the concept of Recursive Coordination Capitalism: an emerging economic regime in which technological progress, human capability formation, corporate investment, and institutional adaptation become recursively aligned through programmable demand architectures. The analysis argues that AI-scale economies intensify coordination problems involving innovation timing, labor transition, adoption velocity, and market concentration. In this context, programmable monetary systems may function as adaptive coordination infrastructures capable of accelerating frontier-technology diffusion, stabilizing long-horizon investment, and partially governing technological transition dynamics. At the same time, the paper emphasizes the institutional risks of programmable economic systems, including surveillance expansion, algorithmic governance failure, and excessive centralization. It therefore proposes constitutional computational governance principles centered on democratic oversight, privacy-preserving computation, transparency, decentralization, reversibility, and institutional contestability. The paper concludes that purpose-bound money is not merely a welfare tool, digital currency, or industrial policy instrument, but a programmable coordination infrastructure capable of recursively aligning technological innovation, corporate growth, and human flourishing in increasingly complex AI-scale economies.
