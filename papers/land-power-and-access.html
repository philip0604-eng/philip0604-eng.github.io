Land, Power, and Access

Renewable Energy Exports, Artificial-Intelligence Infrastructure, and Development without Prior Industrialization

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Global Development19
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper develops and quantitatively disciplines a development strategy in which African countries monetize land-intensive renewable electricity and geographically mobile artificial-intelligence (AI) computation to acquire foreign exchange and expand household access to internationally traded goods and services. The proposal is motivated by a distinction between the capacity to produce a broad domestic manufacturing basket and the institutional ability to command goods already produced somewhere in the world. Building on Lee (2026), Access Over Output, we specify an energy-to-access mechanism linking renewable endowments, foreign offtake contracts, host-country ownership, export receipts, and a rules-based import-access fund interoperable with trade-linked purpose-bound transfers. Two export channels are distinguished: physical power exports to neighboring systems or Europe, and electricity-embodied exports of compute services from African data centers. A spatial multi-technology planning model jointly chooses solar photovoltaics, onshore and offshore wind, geothermal, hydropower, concentrating solar power, bioenergy, marine power, storage, transmission, cooling capacity, and compute placement under stochastic resource, financing, land, reliability, and domestic-electrification constraints. A bargaining and politicaleconomy layer identifies when export rents reach citizens rather than foreign sponsors or local elites. We compile descriptive, source-attributed 2025 installed renewable-capacity observations for 54 African sovereign states; examine a restricted cross-section of electricity-access deficits; and construct transparent, explicitly hypothetical country expansion portfolios and time-resolved dispatch experiments. These exercises are engineering counterfactuals, not causal estimates, observed investment projects, or surveyed national resource potential. The argument is conditional rather than categorical: energy exports can relax an external purchasing-power constraint without requiring prior economy-wide industrialization, but neither land abundance nor zero-fuel-cost generation guarantees competitiveness. Welfare gains require secure land rights, affordable finance, local energy-access floors, dependable fiber and transmission, credible export demand, and enforceable mechanisms that convert resource rents into diversified consumption rights and domestic capabilities.
