Ending Hunger Through Market Design

The Economics of Universal Meal Rights

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

Hunger persists despite unprecedented levels of global food production. In many modern economies, food insecurity is increasingly a problem of access rather than supply. Millions of individuals struggle to secure reliable meals, while restaurants and other food-service providers frequently operate below capacity. This coexistence of unmet nutritional needs and underutilized productive resources suggests a structural market coordination failure. This paper introduces Universal Meal Rights (UMR) and proposes Universal Meal Insurance (UMI) as a new institutional framework for guaranteeing access to ordinary daily meals. Unlike conventional welfare programs based on means-testing, UMI treats meal access as a universal social right. The system is implemented through Meal-Specific Purpose-Oriented Currency (Meal-PoC), a non-transferable, non-accumulable, and automatically expiring monetary instrument redeemable exclusively for ordinary meals. The paper further develops a model of capacity-constrained monetary issuance, in which Meal- PoC creation is linked to the productive capacity of restaurants, cafeterias, and food supply networks. By connecting purchasing power directly to available meal-producing capacity, the framework seeks to expand access while limiting inflationary pressures. More broadly, the paper reframes meals as a form of economic infrastructure rather than a welfare benefit. By combining universal rights, purpose-oriented currency, market-based delivery, and capacity-constrained issuance, it proposes a new market-design approach to food security capable of reducing hunger, improving affordability, and strengthening local food economies in an age of abundance.
