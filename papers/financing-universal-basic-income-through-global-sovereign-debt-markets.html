Financing Universal Basic Income through Global Sovereign Debt Markets

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

Can developing economies turn the demand for reserve-currency sovereign debt into a new and durable source of unconditional household income? This paper develops the original conjecture that a coordinated acquisition of United States Treasury securities can increase the value of previously acquired claims. Rather than relying on recurring sales of those securities, the proposed framework introduces a Sovereign Funding Partnership: an externally capitalized consortium commits verified incremental long-horizon demand, potentially compresses maturity-specific risk premia, and bargains for a legally authorized share of the resulting sovereign financing advantage. A preferred-habitat equilibrium with endogenous supply, cohort-based debt-service valuation, bilateral bargaining, competition among reserve-asset issuers, cooperative allocation, private-information incentives and dynamic reserve safeguards characterize conditions for voluntary participation. An open-economy model links ordinary domestic-currency payments, commercial activity, productive capacity, the external balance and price stability. A liability-matched sovereign ladder, separate liquidity buffer and protected earned-payment schedule stabilize the distributable cash flow without depending on unrealized portfolio marks. Observed Treasury, COFER, EU-Bond and African macroeconomic data and published empirical research provide scale anchors; finite-horizon numerical feasibility calculations and a 20,000-path cash-flow simulation examine the consequences of alternative contractual terms and adverse states. The implementation architecture separates asset ownership from welfare rights and introduces three contingent legal routes: restricted dedicated issuance, an appropriated development matching window and liquidity insurance. Segregated senior mandates, funded first-loss capital, Brady-style debt swaps and legally eligible SDR channels are assessed through participation constraints, cash waterfalls and deterministic stress cases. The accounting and symmetric market-impact impossibility results provide safeguards. The central conclusion is conditional: a genuinely incremental and enforceable funding service can supply external welfare revenue only when its issuer-side value exceeds the coalition’s full opportunity cost and the contract respects external and fiscal constraints. The proposal is an architecture for gradually increasing stable universal income, not a claim that sovereign bond demand alone can permanently finance subsistence at no resource cost.
