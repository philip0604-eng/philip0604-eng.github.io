Purpose-Bound Transfers, Idle Capacity, and Inflation

A Theory of Welfare Provision

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.
Welfare & Capabilities48
01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This paper examines whether restricting public transfers to specified essential services can expand effective access without imposing an equivalent increase in sectoral inflation. It rejects the premise that nonfungibility, by itself, relaxes an economy’s real-resource constraint. Instead, the analysis identifies a narrower mechanism: a payment instrument tied to a particular service may alter the timing and composition of demand, the credibility of provider commitments, and the distribution of capacity investment. The economic case for such an arrangement therefore depends jointly on the household’s inframarginal expenditure, provider entry and spare capacity, the costs of fiscal settlement, and the pass-through of provider revenue into unrestricted money. A two-sector general-equilibrium economy is developed with heterogeneous households, capacity-constrained providers, imperfect competition, and a government that faces a consolidated intertemporal budget constraint. A restricted transfer is shown to be locally equivalent to cash whenever its categorical constraint is slack; when the constraint binds, it can increase desired consumption of the eligible good and raise its price. Under specified conditions, however, verifiable supply contracts and utilization-contingent allocation permit greater real service provision per unit of public expenditure. The paper derives thresholds for price containment, capacity expansion, optimal transfer design, provider rents, and household welfare, including counterexamples where restrictions fail. An explicitly illustrative computational experiment compares four fiscally comparable arrangements over stochastic demand shocks. It is supplemented by a critical synthesis of published evidence on food assistance, retail prices, and program administration, and by a preregistration-ready empirical identification agenda. No observational estimate or simulation is represented as a causal evaluation of the proposed institution. The contribution is a falsifiable theory of when supply-linked purpose-bound transfers may outperform, equal, or underperform conventional cash assistance.
