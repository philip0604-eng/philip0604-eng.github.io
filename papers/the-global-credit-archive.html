The Global Credit Archive

Information Continuity and Financial Stability

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

The institutional architecture of modern credit markets makes a distinction between the failure of a financial intermediary and the preservation of its contractual claims, but it does not invariably make an equally effective distinction between intermediary failure and the survival of borrower information. This paper proposes a Global Credit Archive (GCA): a federated, legally bounded infrastructure through which participating financial institutions preserve verified evidence generated during underwriting, monitoring and servicing, and through which authorized successor and competing lenders may retrieve that evidence under appropriate conditions. The starting point is Bernanke’s (1983) account of the nonmonetary propagation of the Great Depression. The paper extends that argument by treating the portable component of informational capital as an independently maintainable stock, distinct from balance-sheet capacity, contractual ownership and tacit relationship knowledge. A Bayesian underwriting model, an endogenous information-production game, a constrained refinancing market and a dynamic financial accelerator establish conditions under which continuity improves allocation and limits amplification, and conditions under which greater sharing instead discourages screening, synchronizes errors or impairs privacy. The proposed architecture is contrasted with credit bureaus, public credit registries, granular supervisory databases, resolution records transfers and cross-border reporting arrangements. Published empirical research and descriptive U.S. bank- failure statistics from 2001 to 2023 motivate, but do not identify, the proposed treatment effect. A transparent set of numerical experiments quantifies conditional mechanisms, including information-loss recovery, constrained substitution, correlated outage risk and participation incentives. The policy recommendation is an incremental, rights-preserving continuity layer rather than a universal credit score, a global lender or an unrestricted database. The relevant welfare test is whether the marginal social value of verified, usable information exceeds the combined cost of production, governance, privacy risk and systemic concentration.
