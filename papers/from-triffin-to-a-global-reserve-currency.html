From Triffin to a Global Reserve Currency

Joint-Backing Elastic Liquidity and the Design of a Global Reserve Bank

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

International reserve demand is global, while the institutions supporting reserve assets remain predominantly national. This paper develops a Global Reserve Bank issuing settlement balances and reserve securities denominated in an International Dollar. The proposal preserves national currencies and separates international reserve services from domestic monetary stabilization. Its contribution is a contractual framework linking reserve demand, asset pooling, currency-specific liquidity, limited sovereign support, and voluntary participation. A replication benchmark establishes that relabeling existing payoffs adds no opportunity under frictionless access. A pooling result identifies when enforceable transfers expand statewise support capacity without creating aggregate wealth. A reservechoice model derives instrument demand from payment shortfalls, and a constrained issuance problem equates marginal service value with operating, liquidity, and support costs. The analysis distinguishes denomination from backing, liquidity loans from capital, and nominal guarantees from credible contributions. A reproducible numerical illustration using artificial inputs shows that common support can expand feasible issuance while optimal issuance remains below that boundary. Operating costs can reverse the welfare ranking, and an equivalent lower-cost decentralized network can dominate a common issuer. The institutional case is therefore conditional on enforceability, additional service value, and participation. The paper offers a specified alternative for organizing international monetary commitments rather than an empirical estimate of its benefits or a claim that a new currency eliminates national fiscal constraints.
