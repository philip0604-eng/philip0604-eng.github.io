Reprogramming Capitalism

Purpose-Bound Money, Computational Governance, and the Future of Political Economy

DL
Donghyun LeeIndependent Researcher
DL / RESEARCH2026
D/L.

01ABSTRACT

Abstract

This study develops a theoretical framework for layered monetary systems operating through Purpose-Bound Currency (PoC) architectures. It argues that generalized fungible money, while historically effective for industrial-era coordination, structurally transmits financial instability, speculative volatility, and inflationary pressures into capability-generating sectors such as healthcare, education, housing, and nutrition. The paper proposes a differentiated monetary architecture in which selected domains of socially foundational consumption operate through purpose-bound monetary layers partially insulated from speculative financial circulation. By embedding institutional constraints directly into monetary circulation, PoC systems potentially stabilize capability formation while attenuating inflationary and financial-instability transmission mechanisms. Integrating macroeconomics, monetary theory, financial instability theory, capability theory, computational governance, and systems analysis, the study develops dynamic models of segmented monetary circulation, cybernetic stabilization, programmable credit allocation, institutional learning, and computational coordination. It further argues that advances in computation, artificial intelligence, and real-time informational systems fundamentally expand the feasible frontier of institutional coordination beyond the generalized monetary architectures of industrial capitalism. The paper concludes that programmable monetary systems may represent not merely a monetary innovation, but part of a broader transition from industrial market civilization toward computationally coordinated political-economic systems centered on capability stabilization, adaptive governance, and differentiated institutional coordination.
