Reframing Global Fiscal Architecture

A Purpose-Oriented System for Money, Welfare, and Labor

Abstract

This paper proposes a comprehensive redesign of global public finance through the construction of a programmable and supranational fiscal architecture. The framework centers on five institutional innovations: Purpose-Oriented Currencies (PoCs), Purpose- Bound Accounts (PBAs), Luxury-Purpose Currencies (LPCs), Capital-at-Birth mechanisms, and Embassies of Welfare (EoWs). Together, these instruments enable the issuance, allocation, and governance of digital value based on verified social purpose rather than monetary convertibility or discretionary fiscal transfers. Governed by a proposed Global Central Bank (GCB), the architecture decouples the provision of public goods from territorial taxation and aligns resource flows with measurable human outcomes. The proposed system offers a modular response to persistent structural failures in existing welfare, monetary, and international aid regimes. It integrates purpose-linked monetary issuance, life-cycle capital formation, and transnational service delivery into a coherent institutional design. In addition to its core fiscal mechanisms, the framework explores how externalizing income and asset security from employment reshapes labor incentives and innovation dynamics, extending the architecture beyond redistribution toward systemic reorganization. The paper provides a normative justification, institutional roadmap, and formal economic modeling to support the feasibility of purpose-bound issuance at global scale. By reconceptualizing fiscal sovereignty as programmable, functional, and outcomeoriented, the proposed architecture reframes legitimacy in public finance away from territorial exclusivity toward performance and accountability. This research contributes to debates in institutional economics, monetary design, and global redistribution by offering a blueprint for transnational welfare that combines normative coherence with allocative precision.
